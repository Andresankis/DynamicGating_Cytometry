Flow cytometry was performed on a Beckman Coulter Navios EX instrument, which features three lasers (405, 488, and 638 nm), two light scattering detectors, and ten fluorescence detectors. 
To monitor ligand-receptor binding kinetics, reactions were started by introducing a fluorescently labeled antibody into the cell suspension. 
Aliquots (10 µL) were withdrawn at specific intervals for each receptor (e.g., CD14 at 1, 6, 12... min; CD8 at 1, 6, 12... min, etc.) and transferred into 600 µL of PBS to stop the reaction. 
These quenched samples were subsequently analyzed by flow cytometry. 
Cell concentration was quantified in each experiment through the addition of Flow-Count fluorospheres. 
The entire procedure was carried out at room temperature.

The following fluorescently labeled mouse monoclonal antibodies were used: 
anti-CD14 IgG2 (APC; Beckman Coulter, IM2580U, lot 000050, 25 μg/ml), 
anti-CD8 IgG1 (APC; Beckman Coulter, IM2469, 6.25 μg/ml), 
anti-CD16  IgG1 (PE-Cy7; BioLegend, 6607118, lot 7629058, 71 μg/ml), 
and anti-CD11b IgG1 (Brilliant Violet 510®; BioLegend, 301334, lot B238230, 100 μg/ml). 
All antibodies were used at the concentrations provided by the manufacturer for the specific batches listed, as confirmed by a Certificate of Analysis.

All volunteers were from the National Medical Research Center for Circulation Pathology in Novosibirsk, Russia. 
The study was conducted in accordance with the ethical standards of the Declaration of Helsinki.
Peripheral blood was collected from the volunteers into polystyrene tubes containing the anticoagulant potassium ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA). 
The samples were stored vertically at room temperature (24°C) for 30 minutes without agitation to allow for erythrocyte sedimentation.
Subsequently, cells from the upper leukocyte-rich plasma layer were carefully collected and transferred to new tubes containing phosphate-buffered saline (PBS).
